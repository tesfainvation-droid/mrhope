Case 01 · Agents & media

Autonomous AI content studio

An agent system that produces, publishes and improves short-form video on its own.

  • AWS
  • Multi-agent pipeline
  • YouTube APIs
  • Analytics feedback loop

The challenge

Publishing short videos consistently means research, scripting, fact-checking, production, publishing and community replies, every day, plus knowing what actually works.

What I built

An agent system on AWS that researches topics, writes and fact-checks scripts, generates visuals, voice and music, renders the videos and publishes them to two YouTube channels. It also replies to comments.

Highlights

  • Learns from its own analytics through A/B experiments
  • A 48-hour performance score for every video
  • Automatic reallocation of publishing slots toward what performs

For your business: the same research → create → check → publish → measure → adapt loop applies to marketing content, reports, product listings and internal communications.

Case 02 · LLM infrastructure

Multi-provider LLM router

A gateway that keeps AI features running and affordable by spreading work across many model providers.

  • OpenAI-compatible API
  • Google Gemini
  • Groq
  • Cloudflare

The challenge

Relying on a single model provider means one bill, one set of rate limits and one point of failure.

What I built

An OpenAI-compatible gateway: applications talk to one endpoint, and the router routes each request to one of many free and low-cost providers, including Google Gemini, Groq, Cloudflare and others.

Highlights

  • Automatic failover when a provider fails
  • Cooldowns for providers that hit their limits
  • Quota handling across free and low-cost tiers

For your business: lower model costs, less vendor lock-in, and no single provider outage taking your product down.

Case 03 · Agent operations

Scheduled agent “lanes”

A fleet of scheduled AI agents that handle recurring research and business tasks and report back.

  • Scheduled agents
  • Telegram reporting
  • Self-healing
  • Monitoring

The challenge

Recurring research and business tasks are easy to automate once, and hard to keep running reliably week after week.

What I built

Scheduled AI agents, which I call lanes, each with its own research or business job. They report their results to Telegram so a person can see what happened at a glance.

Highlights

  • Each lane runs on its own schedule with a defined task
  • Self-healing behaviour when something breaks
  • Monitoring so failures are visible, not silent

For your business: back-office work that runs on a schedule, with clear reports and a human in the loop.

FAQ

Common questions.

Do you work with businesses in my industry?

Very likely. I work with organisations in any sector. The core techniques, such as agents, automation, retrieval and model routing, transfer well; the first call is where we check whether your use case is a good fit.

Do you work remotely?

Yes. I work remotely with teams worldwide, using email for most communication and video calls at a time that suits both sides.

How do we get started?

Send an email to tesfainvation@gmail.com with a few lines about your organisation and the problem you want to solve. We'll arrange a short call, and if it is a good fit you'll receive a written proposal.

How is pricing set?

Every engagement is scoped individually. You receive a written proposal covering scope, timeline and price before any work begins, and the engagement is governed by a separate written agreement.

Which AI models and tools do you use?

I'm model-agnostic. I've built systems across providers such as Google Gemini, Groq and Cloudflare, and I choose models based on the task, the cost and your data requirements.

Can you connect AI to the systems we already use?

Integration is a core part of the work. The aim is for AI to fit into the tools and processes your team already relies on, not to add another tool to the pile.

How do you handle our data?

How data is accessed, stored and protected is agreed up front and written into the engagement agreement. Systems are designed to use only the data they need.
